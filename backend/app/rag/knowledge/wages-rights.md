## Minimum wage
Every worker has a legal right to at least the minimum wage. Minimum wages in India are fixed by the central government and by each state government, and they differ by state, by type of work and by skill level (unskilled, semi-skilled, skilled, highly skilled). They are revised from time to time. For the exact current rate for your work and area, check your state labour department's website or ask the local labour office. The Code on Wages, 2019 also guarantees equal pay for equal work for men and women.

## When wages must be paid
Under the Code on Wages, 2019:
- Daily-wage workers must be paid at the end of the shift.
- Weekly-wage workers must be paid on the last working day of the week.
- Fortnightly-wage workers must be paid within two days after the fortnight ends.
- Monthly-wage workers must be paid before the 7th day of the next month.

## If your wages are not paid
1. Keep proof: photos at the worksite, attendance, chat messages, call records, and names of co-workers who can be witnesses. On KaamSetu, your job chat and job status history are saved as a record.
2. Ask the employer for payment politely, preferably in writing (a message is fine).
3. If they still do not pay, contact the office of the Labour Commissioner or Labour Inspector in your district and file a complaint. Many states accept complaints online.
4. For free legal help, call the National Legal Services Authority (NALSA) helpline 15100. Legal aid is free for workers who qualify.
5. If you are threatened or attacked, call the police emergency number 112. Women can also call the women helpline 181.

## Safety at work
The employer must give a safe workplace. On construction sites ask for a helmet, safety shoes and a harness when working at height. You can refuse dangerous work without safety equipment. Child labour (children under 14) is illegal.
